## Problem statement

Of the 2,450 firms registered with BCA for general building or civil engineering, 2,085 hold those registrations only in the smallest grades (C1–C3). In our assessment, such firms carry much of a large contractor's paperwork with a handful of office staff. They and their suppliers check bids against tenders, plan how steel and precast cargo is crated and shipped, and write daily reports and safety briefs. By law they must also keep risk-assessment records for at least three years and submit them when the regulator asks.

The costly mistakes are numbers and verdicts: a bid duration that contradicts the tender, a container count guessed from a spreadsheet, a "compliant" that nobody checked, or a safety brief that was signed but not understood. The rise in SME adoption of AI has been driven mainly by off-the-shelf generative-AI tools. Those tools write fluently, but they can invent numbers and state conclusions that no one is accountable for. Businesses also name the cost of adopting new technology and uncertain return as top challenges of transformation.

How might an SME use agentic AI to draft and check this work faster, while code computes every number, missing facts stay visibly blank, a licensed person signs every high-risk document, and the firm's files stay on its own machine or server?

## One-line version

Small construction firms need an AI agent that drafts and checks their tenders, shipping plans and site paperwork without inventing numbers or verdicts: code computes, a licensed person signs, and the firm's files stay with the firm.
